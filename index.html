Problem Statement
Why we're building this

Kirana stores aren't losing to quick-commerce because they won't change. They're losing because no one has time.

This problem statement is built on national industry data and our own in-person field survey of kirana store owners across major areas of Delhi, and in Gurugram, Bahadurgarh, and Dehradun — conducted in August 2026.

Our field research

What shopkeepers actually told us

Delhi, Gurugram, Bahadurgarh & Dehradun — August 2026

Across all four regions, store owners were largely too busy to engage at first — several only opened up after being offered sweets. That reluctance turned out to be data in itself: it's the clearest sign of how stretched for time these shopkeepers already are, city after city.

#1

Labour shortage

The problem named most often, unprompted. Owners can't find hands to run the shop — let alone spare time to learn new systems.

~20%

Revenue lost to quick-commerce

Store owners estimated losing roughly 20% of revenue to Blinkit and Zepto specifically.

#2

Inventory is difficult

Several owners flagged stock-tracking as a real pain point — closely tied to not having enough hands to manage it.

Our problem statement

"Kirana store owners in urban India are losing revenue to quick-commerce not because they resist technology, but because they lack the labour to manage inventory and orders manually — and no existing tool reduces that daily workload rather than adding to it."

Where we start

The narrowest useful thing

1

Stock that updates itself

Inventory decreases automatically as a bill is made — no separate "update stock" task added to an already short-staffed day.

2

Customer ordering, powered by real stock data

Only once stock data is trustworthy do we turn on customer-facing ordering — so the shop never promises something it doesn't have.

3

Nothing new to learn

If it takes training, it adds labour instead of saving it. The bar for every feature: does this remove a manual task, or add one?
